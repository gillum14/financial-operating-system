# Purpose

This document defines how Athena generates, prioritizes, and communicates financial recommendations.

Recommendations represent Athena's primary method of helping users improve their financial confidence.

Every recommendation should be personalized, explainable, actionable, and grounded in Athena's Financial Model.

Recommendations are intended to guide—not control—the user's financial decisions.

---

# Vision

Athena should become a trusted financial guide that consistently helps users identify the next most valuable financial action.

Rather than overwhelming users with every possible optimization, Athena should surface the recommendations most likely to improve their financial confidence while respecting their current circumstances.

---

# Mission

Deliver recommendations that are:

- Personalized
- Explainable
- Timely
- Actionable
- Sustainable

Recommendations should simplify financial decision-making rather than increase complexity.

---

# Recommendation Philosophy

Athena should recommend the **next best decision—not the theoretically perfect one.**

Perfect financial advice often ignores real life.

Athena should recognize:

- competing priorities
- changing life circumstances
- incomplete information
- personal values
- emotional factors
- financial tradeoffs

Recommendations should always balance mathematical optimization with practical sustainability.

---

# Recommendation Principles

Every recommendation should satisfy the following principles.

## Personalized

Recommendations should reflect the user's actual financial situation, goals, household, and priorities.

---

## Explainable

Users should always understand:

- why the recommendation exists
- what information influenced it
- how it supports financial confidence
- what outcome is expected

---

## Actionable

Recommendations should describe a specific action the user can realistically take.

Poor recommendation:

> Spend less.

Better recommendation:

> Reducing restaurant spending by approximately $100 per month would allow you to complete your Emergency Fund Mission three months earlier.

---

## Sustainable

Athena should avoid recommending financial behaviors that are difficult to maintain over time.

Consistency is generally more valuable than temporary perfection.

---

## Respectful

Recommendations should never shame, pressure, or manipulate users.

Athena exists to empower—not judge.

---

# Recommendation Hierarchy

Athena should generally prioritize recommendations according to the Financial Model.

1. Resolve critical financial risks
2. Improve cash flow
3. Build emergency preparedness
4. Reduce harmful debt
5. Protect existing financial stability
6. Improve long-term savings
7. Increase retirement readiness
8. Optimize investments
9. Improve efficiency
10. Pursue advanced financial opportunities

Higher priorities may temporarily override lower priorities.

# Recommendation Priority

Not every recommendation deserves equal attention.

Athena should evaluate priority using multiple factors.

Examples include:

- financial impact
- urgency
- confidence improvement
- implementation difficulty
- user readiness
- time sensitivity
- opportunity cost

Recommendations should clearly communicate priority without creating unnecessary urgency.

---

# Recommendation Confidence

Every recommendation should include an internal Recommendation Confidence score.

This represents Athena's confidence that the recommendation is appropriate given the available information.

Recommendation Confidence should consider:

- data completeness
- historical consistency
- forecast certainty
- financial assumptions
- user behavior
- external uncertainty

High-confidence recommendations should be communicated more assertively.

Lower-confidence recommendations should acknowledge uncertainty.

Example:

> Based on your current spending and savings trends, Athena is highly confident that increasing your emergency fund should be your next financial priority.

Example:

> Athena has moderate confidence that increasing retirement contributions would improve your long-term outlook, though upcoming housing expenses introduce uncertainty.

Athena should never pretend certainty when uncertainty exists.

---

# User Context

Recommendations should adapt to the user's current life.

Context may include:

- household size
- dependents
- employment
- military service
- retirement stage
- disability
- major purchases
- relocation
- income variability
- caregiving
- recent life events

The same recommendation may not be appropriate for two different users.

---

# Opportunity Cost

Financial decisions often compete with one another.

Athena should communicate opportunity costs whenever appropriate.

Example:

> Paying an additional \$500 toward your mortgage this month may delay completion of your Emergency Fund Mission by approximately two months.

Rather than prescribing one option, Athena should help users understand the tradeoffs.

---

# Recommendation Fatigue

Too many recommendations reduce effectiveness.

Athena should:

- prioritize quality over quantity
- avoid repetitive advice
- suppress low-value recommendations
- retire completed recommendations
- adapt when users consistently decline recommendations

The goal is sustained engagement—not constant interruption.

---

# Conflicting Recommendations

Recommendations may occasionally compete.

Example:

- Increase retirement contributions
- Build emergency savings

Rather than hiding the conflict, Athena should explain it.

Example:

> Both options improve your financial position. Based on your current liquidity, Athena recommends strengthening your emergency fund first before increasing retirement contributions.

Transparency builds trust.

---

# Recommendation Lifecycle

Every recommendation progresses through a lifecycle.

| Status      | Meaning                                         |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Identified  | Opportunity detected.                           |
| Recommended | Presented to the user.                          |
| Accepted    | User intends to act.                            |
| In Progress | User has begun making progress.                 |
| Completed   | Recommendation objective achieved.              |
| Deferred    | User intentionally postponed it.                |
| Dismissed   | User chose not to pursue it.                    |
| Replaced    | Superseded by a higher-priority recommendation. |

Recommendations should evolve as the user's financial situation changes.

---

# Confidence Engine Integration

Recommendations should explain how they influence the Confidence Engine.

Whenever possible, Athena should communicate:

- expected Confidence Score impact
- expected Confidence Forecast impact
- affected confidence dimensions
- estimated timeline

Example:

> Completing this recommendation is expected to increase your Confidence Score from 73 to approximately 77 over the next four months.

---

# Mission Engine Integration

Recommendations frequently become Missions.

Examples include:

- Build an emergency fund
- Eliminate credit card debt
- Increase retirement savings
- Reach employer match
- Reduce discretionary spending

The Recommendation Engine identifies opportunities.

The Mission Engine turns those opportunities into structured progress.

---

# Financial Brief Integration

The Financial Brief should summarize recommendation progress.

Examples:

> You completed two recommendations this month.

> Your highest-priority recommendation has remained unchanged because your emergency fund is still below your target.

> Completing your current Mission will unlock additional investment recommendations.

Recommendations should feel connected rather than isolated.

---

# AI Integration

Athena's AI should generate recommendations using the Financial Model and Recommendation Framework.

AI-generated recommendations should remain:

- explainable
- evidence-based
- personalized
- transparent
- adaptable

The AI should never invent financial certainty.

Whenever assumptions influence a recommendation, those assumptions should be disclosed.

Future recommendation capabilities may include:

- business recommendations

- collaborative household recommendations

All future recommendations should continue following the principles established within this framework.

---

# Non-Goals

The Recommendation Framework intentionally does not attempt to:

- Maximize engagement.
- Replace professional financial advice.
- Pressure users into financial decisions.
- Recommend unnecessary financial complexity.
- Optimize solely for investment returns.
- Encourage unhealthy financial sacrifice.
- Hide uncertainty.
- Prioritize Athena's interests over the user's.

Recommendations exist to improve financial confidence—not platform engagement.

---

# Guiding Principle

Every recommendation should answer one question:

> **"Is this the most valuable next step for this user's financial journey?"**

Athena should recommend actions that are practical, transparent, and meaningful—helping users build lasting financial confidence one decision at a time.

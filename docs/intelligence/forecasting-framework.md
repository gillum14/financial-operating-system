# Purpose

This document defines how Athena generates financial forecasts.

Forecasts help users understand where their current financial behaviors are likely to lead if they continue along their present path.

Rather than predicting the future with certainty, Athena estimates plausible financial outcomes based on available information, documented assumptions, and historical trends.

Forecasts should increase understanding, improve planning, and strengthen financial confidence.

---

# Vision

Athena should transform financial forecasting from an intimidating exercise into an accessible planning tool.

Users should understand not only what may happen, but why Athena believes it, what assumptions were made, and what actions could change the outcome.

Forecasts should encourage preparation—not anxiety.

---

# Mission

Provide transparent, explainable, and actionable forecasts that help users make better long-term financial decisions.

Forecasts should inspire confidence by illustrating how today's choices influence tomorrow's opportunities.

---

# Forecasting Philosophy

Forecasts are possibilities, not promises.

Athena should never present projections as guaranteed outcomes.

Instead, forecasts should communicate:

- expected direction
- reasonable probability
- underlying assumptions
- key uncertainties
- opportunities to improve

The objective is informed planning, not false precision.

---

# Core Forecasting Principles

Every forecast should be:

## Transparent

Users should understand how the forecast was generated.

---

## Explainable

Athena should identify the primary drivers behind every projection.

---

## Adaptable

Forecasts should evolve whenever meaningful financial information changes.

---

## Conservative

When uncertainty exists, Athena should favor realistic assumptions over optimistic ones.

---

## Actionable

Forecasts should identify which user actions are most likely to improve future outcomes.

---

# Forecast Inputs

Forecasts may incorporate information such as:

- income history
- spending trends
- recurring expenses
- debt balances
- savings rates
- investment contributions
- retirement accounts
- cash flow
- financial goals
- completed missions
- historical Confidence Scores
- major life events

Every forecast should clearly indicate which data sources influenced the result.

---

# Forecast Horizons

Athena should support multiple planning horizons.

Examples include:

- 30 days
- 90 days
- 6 months
- 1 year
- 5 years
- 10 years
- Retirement
- Financial Independence

Different decisions require different timeframes.

---

# Forecast Confidence

Each forecast should include a Forecast Confidence level.

Forecast Confidence reflects Athena's confidence in the projection—not the user's financial confidence.

Factors influencing Forecast Confidence include:

- data completeness
- historical consistency
- income stability
- recurring spending patterns
- market assumptions
- external uncertainty

Forecast confidence should be communicated clearly and honestly.

---

# Assumptions

Every forecast should disclose meaningful assumptions.

Examples include:

- income remains stable
- recurring expenses continue
- investment return assumptions
- inflation assumptions
- retirement contribution rates
- debt repayment schedules

Changing assumptions should immediately update the forecast.

---

# Scenario Modeling

Users should eventually be able to compare multiple scenarios.

Examples include:

- Increase retirement contributions
- Purchase a home
- Change careers
- Reduce discretionary spending
- Eliminate debt sooner
- Delay retirement
- Receive a salary increase

Scenario modeling should encourage exploration rather than prescribe a single path.

---

# Uncertainty

Financial forecasting always includes uncertainty.

Athena should explicitly acknowledge:

- market volatility
- employment changes
- inflation
- healthcare costs
- tax law changes
- user behavior

Uncertainty should increase transparency rather than reduce trust.

---

# Explainability

Every forecast should answer:

- Why is this forecast changing?
- What assumptions matter most?
- What can I do to improve it?
- Which variables are under my control?

Forecasts should educate as well as inform.

---

# Recommendation Integration

Forecasts should influence recommendation priority.

Examples include:

- projected emergency fund shortfall
- retirement readiness concerns
- cash flow deterioration
- debt payoff delays

Recommendations should explain how they alter future forecasts.

---

# Confidence Engine Integration

The Confidence Forecast is one application of this framework.

Changes in user behavior should influence both:

- projected Confidence Score
- projected financial outcomes

Together, these provide both quantitative and qualitative insight.

---

# AI Integration

Athena's AI should use forecasts to help users explore future possibilities.

AI should clearly distinguish:

- current facts
- forecasted outcomes
- assumptions
- speculation

The AI should never present uncertain projections as guaranteed events.

---

# Future Expansion

Future forecasting capabilities may include:

- tax projections
- education funding
- healthcare planning
- insurance needs
- business forecasting
- estate planning
- household forecasting
- macroeconomic sensitivity analysis

All future forecasting systems should remain consistent with this framework.

---

# Non-Goals

The Forecasting Framework intentionally does not:

- predict markets.
- guarantee financial outcomes.
- replace professional advice.
- optimize for unrealistic assumptions.
- hide uncertainty.
- create fear through worst-case projections.

Forecasts exist to improve planning—not to predict the future with certainty.

---

# Guiding Principle

Every forecast should answer one question:

> **"Based on what Athena knows today, what future is most likely—and what can the user do to improve it?"**

Forecasts should empower users to shape their future rather than simply observe it.

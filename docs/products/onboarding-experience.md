# Purpose

The Onboarding Experience introduces users to Athena by establishing a complete understanding of their financial situation, goals, and priorities.

Rather than functioning as an account setup wizard, onboarding creates the foundation that powers every personalized experience throughout Athena.

A successful onboarding should leave users feeling understood, optimistic, and excited to begin improving their financial confidence.

---

# Mission

Create a personalized financial operating system in less than ten minutes while collecting only the information necessary to deliver immediate value.

---

# Product Philosophy

The onboarding experience is built upon five principles.

## Start with Purpose

Every question should clearly contribute to improving the user's financial experience.

Users should understand why Athena is requesting information.

---

## Reduce Friction

Collect the minimum information required to personalize Athena.

Additional details should be requested naturally over time rather than all at once.

---

## Personalize Immediately

The first dashboard should already feel tailored to the individual.

Users should never receive a generic experience.

---

## Build Confidence Early

The onboarding process should identify strengths alongside opportunities for improvement.

Users should leave feeling hopeful rather than discouraged.

---

## Show Immediate Value

Athena should provide useful insight before onboarding is complete whenever possible.

---

# Onboarding Principles

The onboarding experience should answer three questions.

- Who are you?
- What matters most to you?
- How can Athena help?

Everything else is secondary.

---

# User Journey

The onboarding flow consists of five stages.

## Welcome

Introduce Athena's philosophy and explain what users will gain.

---

## Financial Profile

Collect foundational information such as:

- Household structure
- Employment
- Income frequency
- Financial accounts
- Debt
- Investments

---

## Goal Discovery

Identify the user's priorities.

Examples include:

- Build an emergency fund
- Eliminate debt
- Save for retirement
- Buy a home
- Increase investments
- Reduce financial stress

Multiple goals may be selected.

---

## Personalization

Athena configures:

- Dashboard layout
- Recommended widgets
- Financial Brief preferences
- Initial Mission recommendations

---

## Launch

Present the user's first personalized dashboard.

The experience should conclude with:

- Confidence Score
- First Financial Brief
- Recommended Mission
- Suggested next step

---

# Financial Profile

Athena should establish a complete financial baseline.

Examples include:

- Income
- Expenses
- Savings
- Investments
- Retirement accounts
- Debt obligations
- Financial institutions

Whenever possible, account connections should automate data collection.

---

# Goal Discovery

Goals drive personalization throughout Athena.

Goals influence:

- Dashboard layout
- Mission recommendations
- Confidence weighting
- Financial Brief content
- Widget recommendations

Goals may evolve over time.

---

# Dashboard Personalization

Before users reach the dashboard, Athena should prepare:

- Recommended widgets
- Initial dashboard layout
- Active financial priorities
- Financial Brief
- Confidence overview

The first dashboard should feel intentionally designed for that individual.

---

# Confidence Baseline

Onboarding establishes the user's initial Confidence Score.

Athena should explain:

- Overall score
- Strongest dimensions
- Greatest opportunities
- Immediate next steps

The first score should motivate improvement rather than create anxiety.

---

# First Mission

Every new user should receive a recommended mission.

The recommendation should represent the highest-impact opportunity for increasing financial confidence.

Examples:

- Build a starter emergency fund.
- Pay off a high-interest credit card.
- Connect retirement accounts.
- Categorize recent transactions.

The user remains free to accept, decline, or postpone the recommendation.

---

# Success Criteria

A successful onboarding concludes when the user:

- Understands their Confidence Score.
- Receives their first Financial Brief.
- Has a personalized dashboard.
- Understands their first recommended mission.
- Feels confident using Athena.

# Non-Goals

The onboarding experience intentionally does not attempt to:

- Teach every Athena feature.
- Collect unnecessary personal information.
- Force account connections.
- Delay value until setup is complete.
- Overwhelm users with financial terminology.

Its purpose is to establish trust, understanding, and immediate value.

---

# Guiding Principle

Every onboarding experience should answer one question:

> **"Does this user feel that Athena already understands their financial life?"**

If the answer is no, onboarding should continue to evolve.

Athena's relationship with the user begins here.
